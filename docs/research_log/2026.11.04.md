## Question

How few Genesis episodes does a pelvis-wrench delta need before finetuning against it stops helping? The reference is the best configuration from [2026.10.21](2026.10.21.md): delta + gap gate (λ = 3), randomization off, 10 iterations, which reaches 100% completion and 84.8 mm MPJPE on m4.

**Short answer so far:** a delta trained on 100 episodes (40/30/20/10 from m1/m2/m3/m4, out of the 250 available) is as accurate open-loop as the full-data delta. After finetuning with the gap gate it reaches 100% completion on m4, but global MPJPE is 120.5 mm, against 84.8 mm for the best configuration.

**Nested batch (sections 6–9):** every delta size from 20 to 250 episodes gives 100% completion after finetuning (one exception at 89.8%), and local tracking is the same at all sizes. With the seed-0 deltas, the full 250-episode delta looked best (section 9), but the full grid of section 12 does not confirm it.

**Full grid (section 12):** across 3 delta seeds × 3 finetune seeds, global MPJPE after finetuning shows **no data-scale trend** from 20 to 250 episodes on either clip: +2.0 mm per doubling of data on m4 (95% CI −7.4 to +12.7) and +6.2 mm on m6 (−7.2 to +21.2). Finetuning helps at every size (about 45 mm below the base policy on m4, about 160 mm on m6). The spread comes mostly from the delta (its training randomness alone is as large as the effect of the data draw), then from delta × finetune-seed interaction; the finetune seed by itself barely matters. On the unseen clip m6, nearest-neighbour analysis shows that no training set covers it: 12× more data moves m6 only 4% closer (sections 10–11).

## 11. Nearest-neighbour coverage of m6

- **Method:** for each of the 99,897 m6 state-action tuples (base policy, no noise, `m6_base_seed1_100_onestep.npz`), the distance to the nearest tuple of each seed-0 training set, in the 97 gap-model input features, standardized with the full set's statistics. Reference: held-out trajectories of the training clips against the rest of the full set.
- **Plot:** `m6_nn_distance_raw.png` (distribution per size, and median along the m6 clip).

| Training set | Tuples | Median NN distance (p90) |
|---|---|---|
| Held-out training-clip tuples | – | 0.38 (95th percentile 2.78) |
| 20 episodes | 19.5k | 8.24 (10.64) |
| 40 episodes | 39.0k | 8.15 (10.59) |
| 80 episodes | 78.0k | 8.11 (10.57) |
| 160 episodes | 157k | 7.99 (10.42) |
| 250 episodes | 247k | 7.93 (10.36) |

- **No training set covers m6:** no m6 tuple is as close as 95% of held-out training-clip tuples, and the median distance is about 20× the held-out median.
- **More data barely helps:** 12× more episodes lower the median distance by 4%. The curve along the clip is the same for every size; m6 is farthest around 4.4, 5 and 8.3 s.
- **Nearest neighbours come almost only from m3** (98.4%; the rest from m2), the same subject running. For m6, the amount of running data matters, not the total episode count.
- **The gap is real, not a scaling artifact:** every feature group contributes, joint velocities most (40%), then actions (23%) and joint positions (22%). For scale, m5 (another unseen segment of the same run) has a median distance of 6.7.
- **This fits section 10:** all delta sizes extrapolate equally far to m6, so they finetune to about the same result.

Folder: `logs/delta_finetune/2026.11.04/grid_ds_d012_ft012_gap3_noDR_20261031/` (`run.sh`, `aggregate.py`, `analyze.py`; results in `results.csv`, `summary.csv`, `slopes.json`, `trend.png`).

- **No data-scale trend on either clip.** The intervals overlap at every size, and the 3 delta seeds disagree on the direction: slopes within a delta seed range from −7.7 to +12.8 mm per doubling on m4 and from −7.2 to +21.3 on m6. The data rule out a gain larger than about 7 mm per doubling.
- **Finetuning helps at every size:** about 45 mm below the base policy on m4 and about 160 mm on m6.
- **One delta drives the m4 250-episode point:** with delta seed 1, all three finetunes stay at 171–176 mm, the same as the base policy, as if the delta had no effect. Without it, the 250-episode mean would be about 114 mm. This also explains why the seed-0-only result of section 9 (250 episodes best) does not generalize.
- **Completion drops slightly with more data:** the number of runs with any checkpoint below 95% goes from 1 at 20 episodes to 5 at 250 on m4, and from 3 to 5 at 80–250 on m6.

**Where the spread comes from** (SDs in mm, pooled over sizes; from a 3 × 3 two-way layout per size, so each estimate is rough):

| Source | m4 | m6 |
|---|---|---|
| Delta seed | ~20 | ~26 |
| Finetune seed (same for every delta) | ~2 | ~5 |
| Residual (delta × finetune seed, plus noise) | ~15 | ~32 |
| Same settings run twice (8 m4 pairs) | ~9 | – |

- **The delta is the largest single source.** Its training randomness alone matters: at 250 episodes all three deltas share the same data, and the delta-seed SD there is still about 35 mm on m4 and 25 mm on m6.
- **The finetune seed acts only through the delta:** its main effect is near zero, but the residual is as large as the delta effect, and larger on m6. On m4 it exceeds the ~9 mm of run-to-run noise, so some delta × finetune combinations work and others do not.
- **Isaac finetuning is not bit-reproducible:** rerunning with the same seed changed the checkpoints. This gives the ~9 mm run-to-run SD.
- **For a data-scale test, more delta seeds per size would narrow the intervals more than more finetune seeds.**

## 13. A motion chain toward a hard jump

New direction (2026-11-02): instead of the walk/run chain m1–m6 used so far, pick a hard target clip and build a chain of increasingly hard, relevant clips leading up to it. The new chain is written with an upper-case **M**; the lower-case m1–m6 of sections 1–12 are unchanged.

- **Protocol:** each clip's base policy (`artifacts/checkpoints/lafan_<take>/model_29999.pt`) in Genesis with a 14 kg pelvis, seed 0, 100 rollouts, DR and noise off, startup joint randomization on, plus 1–6 single-rollout videos. Each clip needs its own segment ONNX (`scripts/prepare_segment_artifacts.py`, into `logs/batch_eval/beyondMimic/20261002_214719/segment_artifacts/`): the take-level ONNX embeds the full take, and Genesis reads the reference from the ONNX, not from `--motion_file`. - **The weakest link is M4 → M5** (run to jump, rank 45). Run1 S2 0–20 s was the run clip closest to M5 (distance 0.560, rank 8), but it was dropped from the chain: it completes 62%, about as hard as M4, and is a weak successor to the run clips before it.

Other clips evaluated with the same protocol but not in the chain: Run1 S2 0–20 s (62%, falls at 1.1–3.4 s; the old m5) and Jump1 S1 100–120 s (0%, 4.7 s mean survival).

**Relevance-pipeline brainstorm.** The current matrix ranks the right motion type first, but its contrasts are small (for the old m4, rank 2 and rank 8 sources differ by 0.02) and it mixes style with dynamics. Ideas: a benchmark of hand-labelled triplets (plus structural ones such as same take > same move, other subject > other move); per-feature-group ablations; reference motions instead of executed rollouts; difficulty-weighted or quantile aggregation instead of the mean nearest distance; dynamics features (cadence, flight phase, vertical pelvis oscillation, foot impact speed); weights fitted to the triplets; and, as a transfer ground truth, gap-model uncertainty from one clip on another.

- **Supported claim:** "in Genesis (14 kg pelvis, no DR or noise, randomized initial joints), model_30040 survives the first 5 s of M5 in ≥ 95% of rollouts, at 95% confidence". The ≥ 95% window extends to 6.42 s, but it ends just before the 6.5–7 s cliff, so 5 s leaves a 1.4 s margin. A ≥ 97% claim holds only up to 1.2 s.
- **Reading the bound:** the observed 97.0% at 6 s is only an estimate; the lower bound is the smallest true rate that the data cannot rule out, and a threshold can be claimed only when the bound (not the observed rate) clears it. With zero failures, 59 rollouts certify 95% and 99 certify 97%; at the observed failure rate a 97% claim is not reachable with any N.
- **Most early failures happen right after the start:** 9 of the 13 falls before 5 s occur by 1.2 s, likely from the startup joint randomization. Removing them (a gentler start pose or a settle-in period) and re-certifying on fresh rollouts is the way to a 97% claim.
- **Limits:** failure means a termination check fired; Genesis does not log non-foot contacts or joint-limit violations, so this is not a stricter safety envelope. It is a simulator probability, not a hardware one, and it applies only to this checkpoint.

## Caveats

- Sections 3–5: one training seed per arm, one subsample draw per split, and only model_30010 was evaluated.
- Sections 6–9: each delta seed changes both the episode draw and the training seed, so the two cannot be separated.
- Finetunes: λ = 3 was not recalibrated per gap model. The grid of section 12 has only 3 delta seeds per size, so its intervals are approximate.
- Genesis evaluations occasionally fail with random interpreter errors or write malformed JSON. Both are retried (7 of 270 grid evaluations had to be rerun); no result was affected.
- The m4 open-loop set (σ = 0.2) is dominated by falls.
