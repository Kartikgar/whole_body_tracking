# Research log: week of 2026.10.28

**Status:** best torso policy found (λ = 1.0, randomization off, 20 iterations); one training seed only. The data-scale study continues in the [2026.11.04](2026.11.04.md) log.

## Question

Can a pelvis-wrench delta learned from Genesis, gated by gap-model uncertainty, make Isaac behave like Genesis with an 18.5 kg torso payload? Does finetuning the base policy against it improve Genesis performance?

**Short answer so far:** the delta is accurate on its training data but goes wrong in closed loop. Finetuning against it with a gap-model gate (λ = 1.0, randomization off) raises completion from 56.6% to 99.6% and cuts MPJPE from 175 to 109 mm within 20 iterations. Beyond that the policy over-fits and collapses (49% at 30 iterations).

## 2. Does the delta hold up outside its training data?

- **Held-out open-loop replay** (`m4_torsoEx11.5_delta5600_heldout_5s_20261024/`):
  - Setup: 100 new trajectories, 5 s windows, errors measured every 0.25 s.
  - No generalization gap: body-position error at 5 s is 0.159 m held-out vs 0.148 m on training data.
  - The delta beats zero delta in 99–100% of windows.
- **Closed loop, 500 paired start states** (`m4_torsoEx11.5_delta5600_paired500_20261024/`):
  | Arm | Full-clip completion | Median time to failure |
  |---|---|---|
  | Isaac + delta | 6.6% | 7.4 s |
  | Plain Isaac | 100% | – |
  | Genesis, run A / run B | 56.6% / 54.6% | 12.9 / 13.4 s |
- **Genesis is not reproducible per start state.** Two runs from the same starts agree only at κ ≈ 0.05, but their
completion rates are stable (±4 pp at n = 500). Compare distributions, not individual start states.
- **Conclusion:** the delta is accurate on its training data but goes wrong once the closed-loop policy drifts off it.

## 4. Tuning the gate strength

Folder: `m4_torsoEx11.5_delta5600_lambda_tuning_20261024/`.

- **What was tuned:** λ in the soft gate `delta × exp(−λ·u)`, plus constant output scales c, to match Genesis on
completion and mean survival.
- **Validation:** results were checked on a second set of start states (seed 1).

| Setting | Completion, seed 0 | Completion, seed 1 | Mean delta multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genesis (target) | 55.6% | 54.0% | – |
| λ = 0 (ungated) | 6.6% | – | 1.0 |
| λ = 0.66 | 62.0% | 57.8% | 0.90 |
| λ = 0.75 | 64.0% | – | 0.89 |
| λ = 1 | 86.8% | – | 0.87 |
| c = 0.883 | 62.4% | 57.4% | 0.883 |

- **Matching range:** λ ≈ 0.66–0.75 matches completion and mean survival (15.4–15.8 s vs 16.3 s in Genesis).
- **u adds little here:** a constant scale of 0.883 matches just as well.
- **Failure timing is wrong in every setting:**
  - Median time to failure ≈ 9.5 s, vs ≈ 13 s in Genesis.
  - Only 1–3% of failures fall in Genesis's 12–15 s failure window, vs about half in Genesis.
  - The survival-time distributions differ from Genesis's by KS ≈ 0.24. The run-to-run gap between two Genesis runs is
  only 0.04.

## 6. Randomization off, a no-delta control and a stronger gate (2026-10-26)

All runs used seed 0, delta injection probability 0.5 where the delta is applied, and Genesis with the 18.5 kg torso, 500
rollouts on the paired start states.

- **No-delta control:** the delta is loaded with injection probability 0.
- **Randomization off:** no friction, joint-offset, centre-of-mass or push events, and no observation noise.

| Policy | Iterations | Completion | Mean survival | Median time to failure | MPJPE (mm) | Local MPJPE (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Delta + gate λ = 0.66, randomization on | 20 | 96.6% | 19.68 s | 13.6 s | 139.2 | 34.6 |
| Delta + gate λ = 0.66, randomization on | 40 | 3.8% | 6.48 s | 4.4 s | – | – |
| Delta + gate λ = 0.66, randomization off | 10 | 28.4% | 12.27 s | 6.3 s | 173.0 | 45.5 |
| Delta + gate λ = 0.66, randomization off | 20 | 62.0% | 14.43 s | 4.8 s | 141.7 | 43.0 |

completion.

**Conclusions**

1. **The delta speeds things up but doesn't hold.** With randomization on, it reaches about 97% in 20 iterations, with
   better local tracking than the control at 98 iterations (34.6 vs 39.1 mm). It then collapses by 40 iterations.
2. **Plain finetuning with randomization absorbs most of the payload's effect.** It reaches 99% at about 100 iterations, as
   in the pelvis case ([2026.10.21](2026.10.21.md)). Torso centre-of-mass shifts are especially close to a torso load.
3. **With randomization off, λ = 0.66 is too weak.** Completion swings from 28% to 62% to 36%, and failures come early.
   The torso delta is wrong outside its training data, and randomization partly masked that.
4. **A stronger gate (λ = 1.0) gives the best torso policy so far, but only briefly.** At 20 iterations it completes 99.6%
   with the lowest tracking errors (33.3 mm local, 108.6 mm global MPJPE). It matches the no-delta control's completion
   in a fifth of the iterations and tracks better. By 30 iterations it drops to 49%.
5. **The gate gives no warning of the collapse.** In the λ = 1.0 run, the logged mean gate stays at 0.82–0.84 from start
   to finish, including at 30030. Batch-averaged u does not rise as the policy over-fits, so it can't tell training when
   to stop.
6. **Results swing between nearby checkpoints.** The control went from 79.8% to 99.2% within 8 iterations, so with one
   training seed, compare stretches of checkpoints, not single points.
7. **On the torso payload, the delta is usable only with early stopping chosen in Genesis.** That isn't possible on
   hardware. Two candidates:
   - a stopping signal from u measured along the policy's own rollouts;
   - a better delta, e.g. a generalized force that adds waist torques.
